Granny flat vs tiny home

Short answer

In WA a tiny home on footings is legally a granny flat: it needs a building permit and can be lived in and rented permanently. A tiny home on wheels is a caravan: no building permit, but you can only live in it for 5 nights with the owner's OK, or up to 24 months with council approval.

Last updated 30 October 2026

The key point: a tiny home on footings is a granny flat

In WA the law doesn't have a separate category for "tiny homes". What matters is whether it has wheels:

  • On wheels, it is treated as a caravan, under the Caravan Parks and Camping Grounds rules.
  • On footings on a lot that already has a house, it is an ancillary dwelling, which is a granny flat. On an empty lot it is a single house.

So the real choice is between a granny flat (built on site or delivered as a fixed tiny home) and a tiny home on wheels.

When a granny flat is the better choice

  • Someone will live in it long term, such as a parent or an adult child.
  • You want rental income with no time limit on approval.
  • You want to add lasting value to the property.
  • Your lot fits a 70 m² flat within the standard setbacks, so you can skip planning approval.

When a tiny home on wheels is the better choice

  • You need somewhere to live while you build your main house.
  • The need is temporary, such as a family member staying for a year.
  • You might move, and want to take the home with you.
  • You can't fit a granny flat within the setbacks on your lot.

The middle option

Some people buy a tiny home on a trailer and later take the wheels off and put it on footings. At that point it becomes a dwelling and needs a building permit like any granny flat. It must meet the building code: a proper kitchen, bathroom and laundry, ceiling heights, energy efficiency, engineering and a bushfire report if the lot is bushfire-prone. Many trailer-built tiny homes need changes to pass, so check before you buy if this is the plan.

Questions

Common questions

Is a tiny home a granny flat in WA?

A tiny home on footings on a lot with a house is legally an ancillary dwelling, which is a granny flat. A tiny home on wheels is treated as a caravan instead.

Can I live in a tiny home on wheels in my backyard in WA?

For up to 5 nights with the owner's permission. For longer, you need approval from your council, which can be given for up to 24 months at a time and can come with conditions on power, water and sewage.

Which is cheaper to approve, a granny flat or a tiny home?

A tiny home on wheels needs no building permit, so approval is simpler and cheaper, but it is time-limited. A granny flat needs a building permit and is permanent.

Can I rent out a tiny home on wheels?

Only within what your council approval allows. Ongoing rental of a caravan is generally limited to licensed caravan parks, while a granny flat can be rented to anyone.
