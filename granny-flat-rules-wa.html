WA rules

Granny flat rules in WA

Short answer

In WA a granny flat (ancillary dwelling) can be up to 70 m², on a residential lot of any size, and doesn't need planning approval if it meets the R-Codes' standard rules. Anyone can live in it. A building permit is always required.

Last updated 29 October 2026

What counts as a granny flat?

The planning rules call it an ancillary dwelling: a small, self-contained home on the same lot as a main house. It can have its own kitchen, bathroom and entrance. It is not a separate lot, so it can't be sold on its own.

The main rules

RuleIn summary
SizeUp to 70 m² internal floor area
Lot sizeNo minimum on residential lots since April 2024
Planning approvalNot needed if it meets the R-Codes' standard rules on a residential lot
SetbacksMust meet the normal R-Codes and local setbacks from the boundaries and street

Who can live thereAnyone, and it can be rented out
ParkingSet by the R-Codes and local policy, more flexible since 2024
Building permitAlways required

When you still need planning approval

  • The granny flat is bigger than 70 m².
  • It doesn't meet the setbacks or other standard rules.
  • The lot is in a rural or special zone, or your council's scheme says so.
  • The lot is in a heritage area or affected by special controls.

Other things that can apply

  • Bushfire-prone areas need a Bushfire Attack Level (BAL) assessment, and construction to suit.
  • Water and sewer. Connections need the Water Corporation's approval, and you can't build over sewer lines without it.
  • Strata lots and estates can have their own restrictions on the title.

Questions

Common questions

Is the 70 m² measured inside or outside?

It is the internal floor area of the granny flat, measured inside the external walls. Verandahs and decks are not counted in that figure, but they still count towards site cover and setbacks.

Can a granny flat be two storeys?

It can, but it must still be within 70 m² and meet the setback, height and privacy rules. Upper floors often trigger privacy setbacks, which can mean planning approval.

Can I convert my garage or shed into a granny flat?

Sometimes. It has to meet the rules for a dwelling, including insulation, ventilation, fire separation and energy efficiency, and it needs a building permit for the change of use.
