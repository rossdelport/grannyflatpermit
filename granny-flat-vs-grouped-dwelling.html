Granny flat vs grouped dwelling

Short answer

A granny flat is a small extra home (usually up to 70 m²) that goes with the main house and often needs no planning approval if it meets the R-Codes. A grouped dwelling is a second full home on the lot and always needs planning approval. Both need a building permit.

Last updated 27 November 2026

What's the difference?

  • Granny flat (ancillary dwelling): a small extra home that goes with the main house on the same lot. It can be detached, attached or built into the house.
  • Grouped dwelling: two or more homes on the same lot, where none sits above another. Homes on a survey strata lot with common property also count as grouped dwellings.

Which one suits you?

  • Granny flat if you want extra space for family or a tenant, and plan to keep the property as one lot. It's usually the faster path, because a compliant granny flat often skips planning approval. Try the size calculator.
  • Grouped dwelling if you want a bigger second home, or plan to subdivide and sell. Your lot must have a zoning and size that allows a second dwelling. See minimum lot sizes and subdividing a granny flat.

Councils vary, so check your zoning and local planning policies before you design. A planning officer can tell you which path your lot allows.

Questions

Common questions

What is a grouped dwelling in WA?

Two or more homes on the same lot where none sits above another. Homes on a survey strata lot with common property also count as grouped dwellings.

Does a grouped dwelling need planning approval?

Yes. The City of Canning says planning approval is required in all cases where grouped dwellings are proposed.

Does a granny flat need planning approval?

Often not, if it meets the R-Codes deemed-to-comply rules for ancillary dwellings, including the size limit, usually 70 m².

Is a granny flat or a grouped dwelling quicker to approve?

A compliant granny flat is usually quicker, because it often needs only a building permit, while a grouped dwelling always needs planning approval first.
