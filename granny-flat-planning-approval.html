How to get approval when your granny flat breaks the rules

Short answer

If your granny flat breaks a standard R-Codes rule (such as size, setbacks or parking), apply for planning approval. Show how the design still meets the R-Codes' design principles. Councils have 60 days to decide, or 90 with neighbour comment. Then apply for the building permit.

Last updated 19 November 2026

When you need planning approval

A granny flat that meets all the R-Codes' standard ("deemed-to-comply") rules usually doesn't need planning approval. You need it when your design breaks one of them, for example:

  • It's bigger than 70 m².
  • It's too close to a boundary or sits in the street setback.
  • There's no extra parking bay, and you're not near high-frequency transport.
  • It overlooks neighbours from an upper floor or deck.
  • It's on land with a local plan, heritage listing or rural zoning that adds rules.

Breaking a standard rule doesn't mean a "no". The council checks your design against the R-Codes' design principles instead, which are more flexible.

Step by step

StepWhat to do
1. Find the problemList each rule your design breaks. Some councils offer a paid check to tell you.
2. Explain why it worksFor each one, write how your design still meets the design principle, such as no overshadowing or privacy screening.
3. Lodge the applicationLodge online with the form, plans and fee. A planning fee for work under $50,000 is $147 in 2025/26. Bigger jobs cost more.
4. Neighbour commentIf the change affects neighbours, the council may ask them for comment.
5. DecisionCouncils have 60 days, or 90 days with neighbour comment. Approval may come with conditions.
6. Building permitOnly then apply for the building permit, meeting any conditions.

If the council asks for more information, reply quickly. The City of Gosnells, for example, gives you 21 days or it may refuse. Applying after you've already built costs more: the City of Joondalup adds a penalty of twice the fee.

If it's bigger than 70 m²

Over 70 m² is outside the granny flat rules, so the council may assess it as a second house instead. That brings in lot size and density rules, so ask your council early. Councils vary.

Check if you can skip planning approval, use the granny flat size calculator, or read the full granny flat approval process.

Questions

Common questions

Can I build a granny flat that breaks the rules in WA?

Often yes, with planning approval. The council checks your design against the R-Codes' design principles, which are more flexible than the standard rules.

How long does planning approval for a granny flat take?

Councils have 60 days, or 90 days if neighbours are asked for comment.

How much does planning approval for a granny flat cost?

Council planning fees are set by the state. For work under $50,000 it's $147 in 2025/26. Bigger jobs cost more, and plans and reports add to that.

What if my granny flat is bigger than 70 m²?

It's outside the granny flat rules, so the council may assess it as a second house, with lot size and density rules.
