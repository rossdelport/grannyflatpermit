Granny flat vs house extension

Short answer

Both need a building permit, and neither usually needs planning approval if it meets the R-Codes. A granny flat is a separate home of up to 70 m² that you can rent to anyone, but usually needs an extra parking bay. An extension has no 70 m² cap but stays part of the house.

Approvals: much the same

Both need a building permit. Neither needs planning approval if it meets the R-Codes deemed-to-comply rules and council policy, and the lot isn't heritage listed. The City of Melville, for example, says a single house that meets the R-Codes needs no planning approval.

The extra rules for a granny flat: it must be no more than 70 m², sit behind the street setback, and leave the house with enough open space. Under the R-Codes, a granny flat on most house lots needs one extra parking bay, unless it's within 800 m walk of a train station on a frequent line or 250 m of a frequent bus stop.

Open space applies to both

Whatever you build, you must keep enough of the lot open. The City of Melville quotes 50% open space for R15 to R25 lots and 45% for R30 to R40. A big extension or a granny flat can hit this limit first.

Which suits you?

  • Granny flat if you want a separate home for family, a tenant, or privacy.
  • Extension if you want more space in the house itself, or more than 70 m².

Advertised granny flat prices in Perth start from about $119,000 for a panel build (Fox) and can go over $275,000. We haven't included extension prices, because we couldn't find reliable published figures. See granny flat costs in Perth, granny flat rules in WA or subdividing a granny flat.

Questions

Common questions

Is a granny flat or an extension easier to get approved?

They're similar. Both need a building permit, and neither needs planning approval if it meets the R-Codes. A granny flat has extra rules: 70 m², behind the street setback, and usually a parking bay.

Can I rent out an extension?

Only as part of the house. A granny flat can be rented separately to anyone, including people unrelated to the household.

Can a granny flat be bigger than 70 m²?

Not under the deemed-to-comply rules. Bigger needs planning approval, and the council may refuse it. If you need more space, an extension may suit better.

Does a house extension need a parking bay?

The R-Codes extra bay rule is for granny flats. An extension doesn't trigger it, but check the house still keeps its required parking.
